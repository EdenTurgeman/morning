# 02 — Design brief

**This is the main document. The port exists because of it.**

The behaviour of this app is finished and works. What it is missing is the last
40% of craft that a web app in a home-screen shortcut could never reach: real
haptics, 120Hz motion, GPU shading, depth and material, a rest timer on the Lock
Screen, transitions that carry an object from one screen to the next instead of
cross-fading between two pictures of it.

Your mandate is to make this **the nicest-feeling app on the user's phone.** Not
"a tidy port". If a screen ends up looking like the web version, you have almost
certainly under-reached.

---

## 1. Your mandate, stated plainly

- **Behaviour is fixed.** `04-rules.md` is binding.
- **Content is fixed.** Exercise names, cues, targets, celebration copy, card
  text — port verbatim.
- **Everything else is yours.** Layout, hierarchy, colour, type, material,
  motion, sound, haptics, iconography, illustration, screen structure, and which
  screens exist at all.

You may keep the "morning / sunrise" theme, evolve it, or replace it entirely.
The one thing you may not do is replace it with *nothing*. Read §3 before you
decide.

---

## 2. The bar

Concretely, before you consider a screen finished:

- It holds up next to Things 3, Flighty, Halide or Oura — not next to a typical
  workout app.
- Every state change is animated with intent; nothing pops or cross-fades by
  default.
- Every meaningful tap has a haptic that is *matched to the visual*, not a
  generic `.impact(.light)` sprinkled everywhere.
- It runs at 120Hz on ProMotion with no dropped frames during a timer.
- Nothing scrolls that shouldn't. During a workout, nothing scrolls at all.
- It reads correctly at arm's length, in a dark room, with one eye open.

---

## 3. The idea the current design is built on

Worth understanding before you decide whether to keep it, because it is a real
idea and not just a colour scheme.

> **The app's entire colour is a function of how far through the session you
> are.** It starts at astronomical twilight — deep indigo — and walks the actual
> phases of a dawn as you work, arriving at gold as you finish. The timer ring,
> the progress bar, the primary button, the background sky and the status bar
> all sample that same live value.

The consequence: you can tell how far through the workout you are **from across
the room, without reading anything**. And the session literally ends at sunrise,
which is when it is happening. The completion screen is that sun finally
clearing the horizon.

The implementation is in `src/lib/sunrise.ts` — five hand-picked OKLCH stops,
interpolated perceptually, deliberately not generated by a formula because "a
formula gave an even ramp; it did not give a sunrise."

**If you keep this**, iOS 18 will let you do it far better than CSS could:
`MeshGradient` for a real sky, a Metal shader (`.colorEffect` / `.layerEffect`)
for atmospheric scatter and grain, `Color.mix(_:by:in: .perceptual)` for the
ramp, and a sun that can actually be lit rather than faked with radial
gradients. Budget two passes — your first attempt *will* look worse than the
deployed CSS version, because that one has had four rounds of iteration.

**If you replace it**, replace it with an equally load-bearing idea: something
that makes progress legible without text, that belongs to this specific app, and
that gives the finish a place to arrive at. A palette is not an idea.

## 4. The research pass — do this before designing anything

Study real, shipped iOS interfaces before designing. Use evidence that can be
inspected publicly: official product pages and help documentation, App Store
listing creatives, public demos and walkthroughs, Apple design profiles, and
platform documentation. The user explicitly chose this method after the
available screen-library MCPs required paid plans. This is not optional garnish;
it is the step that separates a competent redesign from a generic one.

### How to work with it

Use only what the public evidence actually establishes. App Store marketing
screenshots prove composition and visible hierarchy, not interaction; demos and
documentation are required for behavioural claims. **Write down what you learn
in `research-notes.md` in this folder** before you open Xcode. You are looking
for *mechanics and patterns*, not pictures to copy.

### What to search for, and what question each answers

| Search around | The question you are answering |
|---|---|
| Workout timers, interval trainers, HIIT, rest timers — Hevy, Strong, Ladder, Fitness+, Peloton, Caliber, Runna | How do the good ones present a countdown you look at from two metres away? What is on screen during a rest vs. during a set? |
| Meditation and ambient — Calm, Headspace, Endel, Oak, Portal, Balance | This is the closest genre for atmosphere, gradient work, and screens that are calm at 6am. Endel and Portal especially for generative colour. |
| Habit and streak apps *without* gamification — Streaks, Reflect, Gentler Streak, Structured | How to show consistency without points and badges. Streaks in particular gets a lot out of very little. |
| Data-as-identity — Oura, Whoop, Strava Year in Sport, Athlytic, Bevel, Cardiogram | The Ledger and The Year screens live here. How do you make a personal number feel like an achievement rather than a dashboard? |
| Flashcards and learning — Anki clients, Brainscape, Quizlet, Duolingo | The study card. Reveal mechanics, card materials, how a question→answer transition can feel like something turning over rather than text appearing. |
| Craft benchmarks in any category — Things 3, Bear, Flighty, Halide, Arc Search, Dark Noise, Overcast | Transitions, custom controls, restraint, and what "premium" actually looks like in 2026 rather than in a dribbble shot. |
| Live Activities and Dynamic Island — Flighty, Structured, delivery apps | The rest timer belongs here. See `05-platform.md`. |
| Onboarding-free / single-purpose apps | This app has one user and no onboarding. Most patterns you'll find assume otherwise — filter them out. |

### What to extract

For each screen you study, note:

1. **The mechanic**, not the skin. "The timer number scales down and slides into
   the nav bar when you scroll" is useful. "It's dark with an orange accent" is
   not.
2. **What they left out.** The best of these apps are defined by what is absent.
3. **How they handle the hard case** — long text, small numbers, first run, the
   state where there is no data yet.
4. **One thing you would steal and one thing you would not.**

### The rule

Extract patterns; never ship someone's brand. No copied icon sets, no lifted
illustrations, no imitation of another app's identity. What you take is
*understanding of what works*.

## 5. Directions to prototype

Build **two or three** of these as real, running screens — not static mockups —
and let the user choose. The set screen and the rest screen are the ones to
prototype, because they are 80% of the app's on-screen time.

**A · Dawn, done properly.** Keep the sunrise idea and execute it at native
quality: a real `MeshGradient` sky with drifting cloud layers, a sun with actual
light falloff, colour that walks the dawn as you work. Warm, atmospheric,
cinematic. The risk is mush — atmosphere fighting legibility at 6am. The reward
is that nothing else on the phone looks like it.

**B · Instrument.** Near-black, one luminous accent, precise type, hairlines,
tabular numerals, mechanical motion. It should feel like a well-made piece of
lab or studio equipment — Halide, a Braun timer, a Teenage Engineering panel.
Progress reads as illumination and precision rather than as colour temperature.
The risk is coldness; counter it with exceptional motion and haptics.

**C · Physical.** Lean into iOS's material layer: depth, translucency,
specularity, elements that have weight and cast light on each other. The rep
counter is an object you move, not a number you increment. The rest ring is
lit glass. The risk is fussiness and dated skeuomorphism; the reward is that it
feels like something you are *holding*.

You are not limited to these. If the research pass turns up something better,
propose it — with reasoning.

## 6. Constraints that survive any direction

These come from `01-product.md` and are not aesthetic preferences:

- Dark by default. It is used before sunrise. A light mode is optional and low
  priority.
- Primary targets ≥ 64pt. The rep controls get 78pt, because they are hit with a
  knuckle.
- Exercise name and rep count legible at 1.5m. Test this literally: build it,
  hold the phone at arm's length, then double that distance.
- Text contrast at least 4.5:1 against its background at every level, including
  tertiary labels. The current palette holds 18:1 / 10:1 / 6.6:1 for its three
  text levels — match or beat that.
- No screen inside a workout scrolls. Ever. If content doesn't fit, the design
  is wrong, not the screen.
- Tabular numerals on anything that changes in place.
- One accent for progress and primary action; semantic colours for success and
  destruction kept off the accent ramp so they never collide with it.
- Respect Reduce Motion. Every animation needs a defined reduced form — not
  "disabled", but a calmer version that still communicates the state change.
- Dynamic Type support at least through the accessibility sizes for reading
  screens (Guide, cards, History). Workout screens may use fixed large type;
  they are already at the top of the scale.

## 7. What "much better" means on this hardware

Specific opportunities the web version could not touch. Use them deliberately —
each one should earn its place, not be checked off a list.

**Motion.** `matchedGeometryEffect` so a set's exercise name *becomes* the
heading of the next screen rather than cross-fading. Spring physics with real
mass rather than eased curves. `PhaseAnimator` / `KeyframeAnimator` for the
Daybreak choreography, which currently runs on hand-timed CSS. `TimelineView(.animation)`
for the timer ring so it advances every frame at 120Hz.

**Haptics.** *This is the single biggest felt upgrade available.* The web build
has **no haptics at all on iOS** — `buzz()` is a silent no-op. Every tap in this
app is currently mute to the hand. With CoreHaptics you can give the rep counter
a detent that clicks as it passes last time's number, the countdown a physical
pulse on each of the last five seconds, and the finish a proper transient +
continuous pattern. Design the haptics as carefully as the visuals; they are
half of how an app feels and this one has none.

**Material and light.** Metal shaders through SwiftUI's `.colorEffect`,
`.distortionEffect` and `.layerEffect` — for the sky, for grain that stops
gradients banding on OLED, for a genuine glow rather than a blurred div. Real
blur materials. Depth that responds to device motion, if it can be done without
becoming a gimmick.

**Rendering.** `Canvas` and `SpriteKit` where they win: the confetti burst, the
year grid, the exercise figures. The current figures are SVG stick figures
animated with SMIL and the user has said twice that they need work — this is
your chance. Consider Rive for them, or hand-drawn `Path` interpolation with a
custom `Animatable`. They should look like a body, not a diagram.

**System integration.** A rest timer on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic
Island, a home-screen widget showing the week, and the session written to
HealthKit. See `05-platform.md`.

**Type.** SF Pro's optical sizes and width variants, `.monospacedDigit()`,
`.contentTransition(.numericText())` for counters that roll rather than swap.
The web build shipped Inter because SF is not available to web apps — you now
have the real thing, tuned for exactly this.

## 8. Screen inventory — what each one is *for*

Function is fixed. Form is entirely yours, including merging or splitting these.

**Home.** Answers "what am I doing and what do I set up?" in under two seconds.
Which session is next (auto-derived), the working weight and the plate breakdown
for it, one large start control, a quiet way to start the other session instead,
where you are in the week, and a way into History / Ledger / Guide / Backup.
Currently a stack of cards; it does not have to be.

**Warm-up.** A 90-second countdown with three instructions. The least important
screen in the app.

**Set.** *The most important screen.* Exercise name, sub-label, load, "set 2 of
3", superset position, the form cues (with the ones carrying the training effect
emphasised), the target range, the rep counter pre-filled from last time, last
time's number, and one primary action. Everything above competes for the same
space — hierarchy here is the single hardest design problem in the app, and the
web version's answer (fixed header, scrollable middle, pinned controls) is a
compromise forced by the DOM, not a good idea to inherit.

**Rest.** A countdown you can read from two metres. What is coming next. A way
to add time and a way to skip. On long rests, a study card. See `04-rules.md`
for the dosing rules — they are deliberate.

**Daybreak.** The completion moment, over the summary. Currently a 4.4-second
choreographed sunrise with confetti. Read the comment at the top of
`src/components/Daybreak.tsx` before you redesign it — it documents exactly why
each beat exists, and the reasoning survives even if the visuals don't.

**Summary.** What you just did, how it compares, where the week stands, one
study card, and out. The comparison copy is generated by tiered rules —
`04-rules.md` — and the copy is fixed.

**History.** Reverse-chronological sessions, a week strip, a year grid, and
deletion behind an explicit edit mode (never a swipe — sweaty hands, and an
accidental delete is unrecoverable).

**Ledger.** Everything ever: tonnage, reps, sessions, hours, and the next
threshold. One staggering true number at the top. This is the screen that exists
to make the last six months feel like they happened.

**Guide.** Static reference read maybe monthly. Nine short entries.

**Backup.** Export to a file / share sheet, restore, erase. With iCloud in play
this may become much quieter — see `05-platform.md`.

### Day one has no data

The app ships starting from zero; the existing history stays in the web app and
is imported later. So the **empty and near-empty states are what the user
actually sees first**, not an edge case to bolt on at the end.

- No last session, no streak, no lifetime figures, and — for one session per slot
  — no "last time" number, which is the app's whole emotional mechanic missing.
- History, Ledger and The Year are genuinely empty. "0 tonnes" is a bad answer
  and so is hiding the screen. Design something that reads as *the beginning of a
  record*.
- The `first` celebration tier — *"You started."* — fires exactly once, ever.
  It deserves real attention for something that will be seen a single time.

Build a debug seeder early (`06-data.md` §2) so you can also design these screens
at six months and a year, where they have to look right eventually. Review every
screen at **empty, one week and six months** before calling it done.

## 9. Motion and haptic doctrine

- **Fast where it's in the way, slow where it's the point.** Step transitions
  should feel instant — you are mid-workout. The completion moment can take four
  seconds because it is the reward.
- **Nothing blocks input.** A transition must never gate the next tap on an
  animation finishing. The web version had exactly this bug and it made the app
  feel broken.
- **Motion carries meaning.** When something gets smaller, it should be because
  it is giving its space to something else. There is a good example already: on
  a rest screen the timer ring halves when a study card's answer appears, and
  that motion *is* the explanation.
- **Every animation has a reduced-motion form.** Not "off" — calmer.
- **Haptics are choreographed with visuals, never sprinkled.** Write them down as
  a table alongside the animation timings.
- **Sound and haptics say different things.** Sound is for events you might not
  be looking at (time is up). Haptics are for confirming something you just did.

---

## 10. Do not inherit these

Artefacts of the web build. Recognise them in the source and leave them behind.

- The three-band "fixed header / scrolling middle / pinned footer" layout on
  workout screens. It exists because the DOM could not guarantee a button stayed
  on screen. SwiftUI can. Design the set screen freely.
- `100dvh`, `-webkit-fill-available`, body scroll-locking, an inner scroll
  container. All of it is dead.
- Manual safe-area padding arithmetic.
- The status-bar colour being kept in sync with the top of the sky by hand.
- `overflow: clip` on the completion sunburst — a workaround for how DOM
  scrollable overflow works.
- Synchronous state mirrors and StrictMode double-render guards.
- Bundle-size discipline. There is no 500KB budget any more: ship real audio
  files, real illustration, a Rive animation, whatever raises the quality.
- The "no dependencies, no build step" rule in the old `spec.md`. That was
  written when the target was a single HTML file. It is void.

---

## 11. Deliverables, in order

1. `research-notes.md` — what you learned from the screen research and what
   you're taking from it.
2. Two or three running direction prototypes of the **set** and **rest** screens.
   Real code, on device, with motion and haptics — not static images.
3. Agreement with the user on a direction.
4. A small design system: colour ramp, type scale, spacing, materials, motion
   curves, haptic vocabulary. Written down, in one file, the way
   `src/index.css` currently documents the web one.
5. The app, screen by screen, in this order: **Set → Rest → Home → Summary +
   Daybreak → History → Ledger → Guide → Backup.** Set and Rest first because
   they are the app; if they are not excellent, nothing else matters.

## 12. Before you call a screen done

- [ ] Does it beat the web version, obviously, to someone glancing at both?
- [ ] Held at arm's length in a dark room, is the one thing you need to know the
      first thing you see?
- [ ] Does every tap answer in the hand as well as on screen?
- [ ] Does anything scroll that shouldn't?
- [ ] Does the longest possible content still fit — longest exercise name,
      four cues, a three-line question with a seven-line answer?
- [ ] Does it hold at **empty**, at one week, and at six months of data?
- [ ] Does it hold at the largest Dynamic Type size it claims to support?
- [ ] Does Reduce Motion produce a calmer version rather than a broken one?
- [ ] Is there any copy that congratulates without saying something true?
- [ ] 120Hz, no dropped frames, during a running timer?
